Except for Palestine

Marc Lamont Hill and Mitchell Plitnick (2021)

Central argument

American progressives frequently defend civil rights, self-determination, and international law while making exceptions for Israeli policies toward Palestinians. Bipartisan military assistance, diplomatic protection, and restrictions on political boycotts have helped sustain Israeli control over Palestinian territory. These contradictions predate Donald Trump and persist within the Democratic Party.

Mechanisms and evidence

1. Recognition and Palestinian statehood

  • In 1975, Henry Kissinger committed the United States to withholding recognition of the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) until it recognized Israel and accepted UN Resolutions 242 and 338.

  • The PLO recognized Israel in 1988. Under the 1993 Oslo Accords, Israel recognized the PLO as the Palestinian representative but did not recognize Palestinian statehood.

  • Benjamin Netanyahu subsequently emphasized recognition of Israel specifically as a Jewish state, complicating Palestinian claims to equality and refugee return.

  • The authors argue that recognition requirements imposed obligations on Palestinians without equivalent Israeli commitments to sovereignty.

2. Zionism and Palestinian rights

  • Zionism sought Jewish national self-determination in Palestine, where Palestinian Arabs also held national and territorial claims.

  • Ze'ev Jabotinsky argued in The Iron Wall (1923) that Jewish state-building required military superiority because Palestinians would resist displacement.

  • The Nakba displaced hundreds of thousands of Palestinians in 1948. Their property claims and Palestinian Right of Return remain unresolved.

  • The authors question whether maintaining a Jewish demographic majority can be reconciled with equal Palestinian rights.

3. BDS and free speech

  • Palestinian organizations launched Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions (BDS) in 2005, demanding an end to occupation, equal citizenship, and refugee rights.

  • The movement followed the 2004 International Court of Justice opinion finding Israel's separation barrier in occupied territory contrary to international law.

  • Numerous American states passed anti-BDS laws restricting public contracts with boycott participants.

  • NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware Co. (1982) established constitutional protections for political boycotts. Federal courts blocked several anti-BDS restrictions.

  • The authors distinguish criticism of Israeli policies from Antisemitism, arguing that accusations of prejudice can be used to suppress legitimate political opposition.

4. Trump's policies and bipartisan precedents

  • Israel captured the Golan Heights in 1967 and extended its laws there in 1981. UN Security Council Resolution 497 rejected the annexation. Trump recognized Israeli sovereignty in 2019.

  • The Jerusalem Embassy Act (1995) directed relocation of the American embassy. Clinton, Bush, and Obama postponed implementation; Trump moved it in 2018.

  • In 2018, Trump ended American funding for UNRWA, which provides education, health care, and assistance to Palestinian refugees.

  • The authors argue that Trump expanded longstanding American support for Israeli territorial claims rather than creating that support independently.

5. Gaza disengagement and blockade

  • Under Ariel Sharon, Israel removed settlements and permanent ground forces from Gaza in 2005 but retained substantial control over borders, airspace, and maritime access.

  • Hamas won the 2006 Palestinian legislative elections. The United States imposed diplomatic and financial pressure while supporting rival Fatah forces associated with Mahmoud Abbas and Mohammed Dahlan.

  • Hamas seized control of Gaza following armed conflict with Fatah in 2007.

  • Israel and Egypt subsequently restricted Gaza's movement and trade. Restrictions, military destruction, and political division contributed to unemployment, infrastructure deterioration, and humanitarian dependency.

  • The authors argue that disengagement reduced Israel's administrative responsibilities without establishing Palestinian sovereignty.

6. Military operations and accountability

  • Operation Cast Lead (2008–2009) caused extensive destruction in Gaza. The Goldstone Report investigated alleged violations by Israeli forces and Palestinian armed groups.

  • Operation Protective Edge (2014) produced further civilian casualties and infrastructure destruction.

  • During the Great March of Return (2018), Israeli forces shot Palestinian demonstrators near Gaza's separation barrier.

  • The authors argue that American diplomatic protection and military assistance reduced pressure for accountability despite repeated civilian casualties.

7. Progressive political opposition

  • Bernie Sanders criticized Israeli military actions during the 2016 Democratic presidential primaries.

  • Rashida Tlaib and Ilhan Omar entered Congress in 2019 and publicly defended Palestinian rights.

  • The authors identify growing Democratic support for criticizing settlements and conditioning assistance, while congressional leadership remained reluctant to impose consequences.

Historical alternative

  • Under the Egypt–Israel Peace Treaty (1979), Israel agreed to withdraw from the Sinai Peninsula in exchange for peace and security arrangements. Withdrawal was completed in 1982.

  • The authors contrast this negotiated withdrawal with continued Israeli control of the Golan Heights and Palestinian territories.

Arguments challenged

  • Palestinians refuse to recognize Israel: The PLO recognized Israel in 1988 without obtaining Palestinian statehood.

  • BDS is inherently antisemitic: Criticism of Israeli state policy is not automatically hostility toward Jews.

  • Anti-BDS laws protect against discrimination: Government contracting restrictions can penalize constitutionally protected political activity.

  • Trump alone transformed American policy: Several policies had bipartisan precedents.

  • Israel ended its occupation of Gaza in 2005: Israel retained substantial external control.

  • Hamas alone caused Gaza's crisis: External restrictions, American intervention, and Palestinian factional conflict also contributed.

  • American progressives consistently defend human rights: The authors identify exceptions involving Palestinian sovereignty, civilian protection, and political expression.

Further questions

  • What explains bipartisan American support for Israel despite disagreements over settlements and Palestinian statehood?

  • How much did American intervention contribute to the 2007 Hamas-Fatah conflict?

  • Which restrictions contributed most to Gaza's economic deterioration?

  • How effective has BDS been compared with the anti-apartheid movement?

  • Why has criticism of Israeli settlements rarely resulted in conditions on American military assistance?
